Study 01

Building Study: The Presidential Towers

My first task was to understand the firm's flagship building. The Presidential Towers sits on about 5 acres on Tumkur Main Road in Yeshwanthpur, Bengaluru, with 352 apartments and 16 sky villas in 3 and 4 BHK layouts, and a 40,000 sq. ft. clubhouse on the 50th floor.

Walking the site taught me more than the brochure. I documented the shear wall core, ceilings with no beams, lightweight concrete blocks for internal walls, Reflectasol glass waiting for installation, and the topmost floor kept for solar panels.

  • Dry wall partitions in lightweight concrete block reduce dead load and make services routing easier.
  • MIVAN aluminium formwork allows monolithic casting of slabs and walls, which shows in the seamless ceiling joints.
  • Putty (patti) is applied every 4 feet vertically to even out block undulations before final coats.
  • Bridges connect the three vertical blocks every sixth floor, adding stability and reducing wind sway.
Study 02

Material Study and Bill of Quantities

I studied three materials used or proposed in the firm's work. Reflectasol by Saint-Gobain is a pyrolytic coated solar control glass used on the tower; its low light transmittance gives visual comfort and privacy while keeping interiors cool. The UNITILE USF 800 EB raised floor uses an edge support rigid grid, with 600 x 600 mm cement-core panels on adjustable pedestals. WPC boards from Tree Lab (Brimi Group) are wood-plastic composites that resist termites, water and weather, though they cost more than wood and contain plastic.

I also prepared a bill of quantities for The Presidential Tower: 18 Jaquar sanitaryware items, 77 pieces in total, priced at ₹6,34,025. Vinod checked it.

Study 03

How to Do a Mood Board

Much of the interior work started with a mood board, so I wrote down the method I learned. A mood board is a visual collage that conveys the style, theme and feel of a concept through images, colours, textures and materials.

I structure it in six steps: keywords for the style, image sets covering space, product, texture, form and lighting, an abstraction of the core idea, a colour ratio, materials for floor, ceiling and decor, and finally furniture chosen by form, style, colour and material. The colour ratio follows 60% primary, 30% secondary and 10% accent, always noted with HEX codes.

My Boho board uses #e4e3e1, #88715a and #535e53 with rattan and linen, layered lighting, soft-geometry furniture and indoor greenery. I later reused it for ZenX.

Study 04

Architectural Details

Detailing showed me how a design is actually put together. I drew a set of interior and joinery details for ZenX, checked by Sonal.

The sheet covers two skirting conditions, a side mount drawer slide with 18 mm MDF and PVC edging, a cabinet top cover with a 45 degree cut handle and door stop, and a staircase detail. The staircase shows the steel plate and intermediate metal post screwed to the floor, with metal cable railings over a reinforced concrete slab. Drawing at this scale made me think about tolerances in centimetres rather than metres.

Project 05

ZenX

ZenX is an eco-friendly company that builds technologically advanced pod spaces, with the line "zen for the next gen". I developed A-frame pod options for them.

Option 1 is a compact 7.5 m x 4 m pod with living, toilet and bedroom in a row, paired with the Boho interior board. Option 2 grows to a 10 m x 6 m footprint with a mezzanine bedroom: 60 sq.m on the ground floor and 27.62 sq.m above, 87.62 sq.m in total.

The front elevation is meant to sit quietly in a forest and use passive solar gain. It has a 45 degree roof in asphalt shingles, glass gables, Canadian Spruce-Pine-Fir cladding panels and a slab-on-grade foundation. I used Midjourney AI to show the pods in their setting, and drew a section through the living, kitchenette and mezzanine bedroom.

Project 06

Carnoustie

Carnoustie is a hospitality company, and this was the project I worked on most deeply. I produced three options, each drawn by me and checked by Sonal.

Options 1 and 2 place a bathroom, staircase and bedroom on the ground floor, a mezzanine bedroom above a double-height cutout, and a zen garden along one side. Option 2 adds a private pool and deck, at 106 sq.m of ground coverage and 123 sq.m in total. Option 3 links two A-frame pods around a shared living area and kitchenette, with a shared open terrace, spiral staircase, garden lounge and pool.

I also detailed the 5.3 m x 10 m outdoor deck: 30 mm Canadian SPF boards on pressure-treated joists, galvanised L-brackets, 300 x 300 mm concrete pad footings, and a planter box with a gravel reservoir and overflow. The planters were approved on site.

Project 07

Millers' Office

Millers' Office was a renovation of a compact 5 m x 18.5 m office. I began with a biophilic minimalism mood board: neutral colours, soft textures, modular felt and glass dividers, terrazzo tiles for the corridor and carpet inside.

The zoning plan balances collaboration and focus. Designers and lawyers each get their own zone, with a conference room, two discussion rooms, a private cabin, a chill zone, a kitchenette and a printer area. A central spine keeps circulation clear along the long, narrow plan.

I finished with staircase details in plan, section and elevation.

Project 08

Rustic Ridges

Rustic Ridges, "escape to the edge", is a ZenX resort near Hampi in Karnataka. I did the site study and a first masterplan.

The site covers 67 acres in two phases of 35 and 32 acres, with a government heliport proposed and a second heliport proposed by ZenX. The land is rocky and boulder-strewn but level, suited to low-impact construction. It lies next to a zoo and sloth bear sanctuary, so development has to be wildlife-friendly and low-noise. The Group of Monuments is 20 minutes (9.4 km) away.

The masterplan places 50 wooden homes around a restaurant, swimming pool and club house, with reception, admin and staff quarters. Tented camping and stargazing, cycling trails, observation towers and a sunset café spread into the landscape.

What I took away

  • Seeing MIVAN formwork, blockwork and putty work on site made construction drawings make sense to me.
  • A BOQ turns a design choice into a number a client can act on.
  • Developing three options for one brief taught me to present choices, not just one answer.
  • Every drawing I produced was checked by a mentor, and that review loop is how I learned to draw for construction.
