Anvaya Youth Center

A five-level youth centre by Allalasandra Lake in Yelahanka, Bengaluru, built around cascading terraces.

School
BMS School of Architecture
Site
Allalasandra Lake, Yelahanka, Bengaluru, Karnataka
Programme
Youth centre with workshops, co-working, library, seminar rooms, dorms and a rooftop café

Anvaya is a youth centre for Yelahanka's growing young population. I designed it as a building of terraces that step down across five levels, so that indoor rooms open onto outdoor space at every floor. The aim was a place for skill-building, leadership and meeting people, where young people feel free to explore and learn.

01

The site

The site sits beside Allalasandra Lake in Yelahanka, Bengaluru. The lake has calm water, lush greenery and a rich ecosystem, which offers some quiet in a busy part of the city.

I studied accessibility, a nearby religious building, the sun path, wind patterns and views. The sun moves east to west and the prevailing wind comes from the southwest. The lake helps cool the site passively, and the trees around it give shade and a cool breeze. I wanted the building to make the most of the views towards the water.

02

A cascading building

The central idea is a cascade. The youth centre fills the two lower levels, dorms take the next two, and a café sits at the top. Co-working blocks sit separately beside a small garden. Each level steps back from the one below, so every floor has its own terrace.

03

Form development

I developed the form in eight steps, starting from a single open void and ending with a balanced hierarchy of spaces. Along the way the void grows into connected transitional areas, then into courtyards on the edges, then into nested and overlapping spaces. The courtyards finally form clusters.

  • Initial formation of a central open void
  • Gradual expansion of spatial boundaries
  • Creation of connected transitional areas
  • Development of defined perimeter courtyards
  • Formation of layered and nested spaces
  • Integration of overlapping and intersecting voids
  • Expansion of courtyards into clustered networks
  • Establishment of balanced spatial hierarchies
04

Plans

The ground floor holds the public functions: a reception, a library, two seminar rooms, workshop spaces and co-working spaces, all arranged around a landscaped plaza with a water channel. The first floor has more workshop and co-working space, balconies, and a bridge between the two wings. The second and third floors are dorm rooms around a service core. The fourth floor is the café, with a kitchen and indoor and outdoor seating.

05

Terraces and the community street

The aerial view shows how the parts come together: green terraces, interactive courtyards and the rooftop café, all visible from each other and from the ground. In section, a community street runs through the base of the building. Above it the floors step back to form the café's outdoor seating and a landscaped terrace, with services and the hostel stacked at the back.

What I took away

  • Stepping the section gave every floor its own outdoor room.
  • Working the form through a clear sequence of steps made the massing easier to explain.
  • Stacking public uses at the base and quieter dorms above kept the programme legible.
