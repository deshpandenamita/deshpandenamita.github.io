Sattvana Resort

An ideation-stage study of three pod types to find the one best suited to a resort in an island setting.

Sattvana is a resort project where I explored three pod types, A-frame pods, modular pods and space pods, to find which one best suits an island site. I compared how each one works, how sustainable it is, and how it could be placed so guests stay connected to the landscape. The project is still at the ideation stage, and I have adapted the designs to reflect my own approach.

01

The brief

The aim of Sattvana is a resort that offers comfort, a sense of calm and some new ideas while staying close to nature. Instead of starting from one building, I started from the guest unit. I asked which kind of pod would sit best in an island landscape, and how pods could be placed across the site to give each guest an immersive stay.

02

A-frame pods

A-frame pods take their triangular geometry from traditional structures. The steep roof sheds rain quickly and helps natural ventilation, so the type works in many climates. Large glazed ends open the interior to the outdoors. The type can use natural materials and simple, low-impact construction, and it fits a lot into a small footprint.

03

Modular pods

The modular, rectangular pod is about minimalism and utility. Its grid-based layout and linear geometry make it easy to stack and transport, so it can scale up. Storage and services are built in to keep the interior uncluttered, and the type can take solar panels and green facades.

04

Space pods

The space pod is the most experimental of the three. It was conceived for extreme environments, with an aerodynamic shell, lightweight composite materials and built-in systems for air and climate control. Its interior balances function and comfort for longer stays, making it the most futuristic of the three options.

05

Developing the pod

I developed the pod through sectional ideation, cutting through it to test how the bathroom, kitchen, dining and sleeping areas could sit in a single line. The floor plan fits an 8 m by 4 m footprint. It has a living room, a kitchenette and dining room, a toilet, a utility space and a bedroom, with the kitchen and utility in the middle so the living room and bedroom each get an end of the pod.

The final render shows the pod as a rounded timber capsule, with full-height glazing along one side so the whole interior looks out to the landscape.

What I took away

  • Comparing pod types side by side made their trade-offs clear.
  • An 8 m by 4 m footprint can hold a complete small home if the services sit in the middle.
  • This project is still at the ideation stage and open to refinement.
